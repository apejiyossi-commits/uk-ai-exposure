# Forecast: young job starts into AI-exposed occupations, next LFS half-years

Written 2026-10-16, before the data exist. Committed to the public repository so the date is fixed.

**Quantity.** The event-study coefficient of Step 6 (Figure 4): the gap in the quarterly job-start
rate of 22-25 year olds between high-exposure and near-zero-exposure jobs, relative to 2022H2,
in percentage points of the age group. Same code, same definitions, new quarterly files only.

**What the data show so far.** Post-2022 half-years: mean -1.08 pp, standard deviation across
half-years 0.36 pp, typical standard error 0.59 pp. The last two half-years average -0.66 pp.

**Forecasts for 2026H2 and for 2027H1** (each a separate half-year):
- If the post-2022 shift is persistent (the AI-demand reading): about -1.08 pp,
  with a 95% prediction band of -2.43 to +0.27 pp.
- If it was transitory (the cyclical or pandemic-recovery reading): about 0, band -1.35 to +1.35 pp.

**Decision rule, fixed now.** Average the 2026H2 and 2027H1 coefficients. Below -0.54 pp
(half the post-2022 mean) counts as evidence for persistence; above it, as evidence that the gap has faded.
Each half-year has a standard error near 0.59 pp, so a single half-year cannot settle this on its own.

**Second, faster check (public, monthly).** The online postings gap of Step 12b (Figure 9),
75th vs 25th percentile of sector exposure, relative to November 2022. Since July 2023 it has
ranged from -7 to -14 log points. Persistence predicts it stays below -5 in every
month from October 2026 to March 2027; a return above -5 would count against it.
